MORDEN2

MORDEN2 is a browser editor for MSX2 sprite mode 2 sprites, built around the one thing other MSX sprite editors don't show you: the OR-color effect between overlapping sprites.

You draw up to 8 sprites of 16×16 pixels, switch any of them to OR mode, and the preview shows exactly what the MSX2 video chip will display when they overlap. When you're done, export the result as BASIC, C, raw binary, PNG or a TinySprite backup.

Everything runs in your browser. Your work is saved automatically on this computer.

MSX2 sprite basics

A few hardware rules shape everything in the editor:

  • 16×16 pixels, one bit per pixel. Each pixel is either transparent or opaque.
  • One color per row. Every opaque pixel in a row shares that row's color. That's why painting a pixel recolors its whole row (see Row colors).
  • At most 8 sprites on one scanline. MORDEN2 caps a project at 8 sprites so whatever you design can really be shown at once.
  • Lower sprite number = higher priority. Where sprites overlap, the leftmost card (Sprite 0) is drawn on top.
  • OR color (the CC bit). A sprite line with the CC bit set has no priority of its own. Where it overlaps the sprite before it, the two color indexes are combined with a bitwise OR, which gives you a third color in that spot. See OR mode.

The full details are in the MSX2 Technical Handbook, chapter 4, section 5.3 "Sprite mode 2" (online, or the copy in this project).

Sprites

Each card is one hardware sprite. Cards are numbered from 0, left to right, and that order matters: it is the priority order on the MSX2 and decides which sprite an OR sprite mixes with. Exports keep the same order.

  • Add sprite: the dashed card at the end adds an empty sprite, up to 8. It shows how many you have.
  • Remove: the × in a card's header deletes it. The cards after it move left and are renumbered. A project always keeps at least one sprite, so the button is hidden when only one is left.
  • Active sprite: the sprite with the blue border and the Active tag. It's the last one you clicked, or Sprite 0 at the start. Paste, Rotate / Flip and Ctrl+A act on it.
  • OR: the header button that switches the sprite's OR mode on and off.

Drawing on a grid

Pick a color in the palette, then click or drag on a grid. What happens depends on the tool chosen in the rail:

  • Pencil paints pixels one by one.
  • Fill fills a connected area.
  • Select marks a rectangle for the clipboard.

Two mouse shortcuts:

  • Right-click erases the pixel under the cursor, whatever the tool.
  • Middle-click is an eyedropper (with Pencil or Fill): it picks that pixel's color (or transparency) as the current color.

When you hover a pixel, the same row and column are highlighted in every sprite and in the preview, which makes it easy to line up pixels that should OR together.

Pencil P

Click to paint one pixel, or drag to paint a stroke. A whole stroke is one undo step.

  • Painting with a color makes the pixel opaque and sets that row's color, so other opaque pixels in the row change color too. That's the one-color-per-row hardware rule, not a bug.
  • Painting with the transparent swatch erases pixels and leaves the row color alone.

Fill F

Click a pixel to fill every pixel connected to it (up, down, left and right) that has the same color. Transparent counts as a color, so clicking an empty area fills it, and clicking a shape recolors it.

  • With the transparent swatch, Fill erases the connected area.
  • Clicking a pixel that already has the current color does nothing.
  • Rows are still single-color: every row the fill touches takes the new color, including pixels in that row outside the filled area.

Select S

Drag on a grid to select a rectangle. The selection is shown in blue and belongs to one sprite at a time.

  • Click a sprite's name in its card header to select the whole sprite.
  • Ctrl+A selects the whole active sprite.
  • Esc clears the selection.

A selection enables Cut, Copy and Clear.

Cut, copy & paste

Cut Ctrl+X and Copy Ctrl+C take the current selection: its pixels plus the colors of the rows it covers. Cut also clears those pixels.

Paste Ctrl+V goes into the active sprite:

  • A whole sprite replaces the active sprite's contents right away, including its OR setting.
  • A rectangle appears as a floating overlay with a yellow frame. Drag it into position, then press Enter or click outside the sprite to place it. Esc cancels. While pasting, the other tools are disabled.

Pasting follows the row color rule: any row where the pasted piece has an opaque pixel takes the copied row's color.

Rotate & flip

These act on the whole active sprite. They are disabled while a rectangle (not the whole sprite) is selected in it.

  • Rotate turns the sprite 90° clockwise. Because rows become columns, each new row takes the color most of its pixels came from.
  • Flip horizontal mirrors left to right. Row colors don't change.
  • Flip vertical mirrors top to bottom. Row colors move with their rows.

Clear

Makes every pixel in the current selection transparent. Row colors are kept, which is harmless because a row with no opaque pixels shows nothing.

Row colors

The narrow column to the right of each grid shows the color of each of its 16 rows, with the palette number. A dashed outline means the row has no color yet.

Click a row's swatch to give that row the current color. Its opaque pixels are recolored and no pixels are added or removed.

This column is exactly what the MSX2 stores in the sprite color table: one byte per row.

OR mode

Turn on the orange OR button on a sprite card to set the CC bit on all 16 of its rows. The card then says which sprite it mixes with.

How the MSX2 handles it:

  • An OR sprite mixes with the nearest sprite before it that is not in OR mode. Several OR sprites in a row all mix with that same sprite, and with each other.
  • Where they overlap, the color indexes are combined bit by bit: for example 8 (1000) OR 7 (0111) = 15 (1111). The OR mixes box shows which pairs give the current color.
  • Where the OR sprite doesn't overlap, it shows its own color.
  • If an OR sprite has no normal sprite before it (for example Sprite 0 in OR mode), there's nothing to mix with. The card warns you, and MORDEN2 draws it as a normal sprite.

Card order decides who mixes with whom. The preview always applies these exact rules. More in the MSX2 Technical Handbook, section 5.3.2.

Color palette

The top of the side panel shows the current color large, with its number, name and hex value. Below it are the 16 swatches:

  • The checkered swatch is transparency: drawing with it erases.
  • Swatches 1–15 are the sprite colors. Click one to make it current.

The palette is fixed, as on the real hardware. You can switch between two fixed palettes with Regular / Screen 8.

Regular / Screen 8

The switch in the top-right corner picks how color numbers 1–15 look:

  • Regular: the standard MSX sprite colors.
  • Screen 8: the fixed colors sprites get in SCREEN 8, which don't follow the regular palette.

Switching only changes how the numbers are displayed and exported to PNG. The sprite data (the color numbers) stays the same. The choice is saved with the project.

OR mixes

The box under the palette lists pairs of colors that OR together into the current color. For example, color 3 can be made from 1 OR 2. Use it to plan a third color with just two sprites.

  • A pair with a green border uses a color already in one of your sprites, so it's usually the cheapest option.
  • The box shows up to 6 pairs so it always keeps the same size. When there are more, More (n) opens a window with all of them. Close it with ×, Esc or by clicking outside.
  • Some colors (such as 1, 2, 4 and 8) have no mix, because they have only one bit set.

Preview

The preview shows all sprites combined the way the MSX2 would draw them, with priority order and OR mixing applied. It updates as you draw.

  • Drag a sprite in the preview to move it relative to the others. OR only happens where sprites overlap, so this is how you control where the mixed color appears. Each drag is one undo step, and the offsets are used by the BASIC and PNG exports.
  • Recenter puts every sprite back at the same position (0, 0).
  • 1× / 1.5× / 2× change the preview size.
  • The pixel you hover in a grid is outlined in green here, at its real position.

Undo & redo

Undo Ctrl+Z and Redo Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) share one history for the whole project. It covers painting, filling, row recoloring, OR toggles, adding and removing sprites, clipboard actions, rotate / flip, preview drags and imports.

  • A whole stroke or drag counts as one step.
  • Clicks that change nothing (like erasing an empty pixel) don't add a step.
  • The history lasts for the current session only. New project clears it.

File menu

New project erases every sprite and starts over with one empty sprite, after asking you to confirm. It also clears the undo history, the clipboard and the autosaved copy, so it can't be undone. Export first if you want to keep your work.

Import

Imported sprites are added after your existing ones, and each import is one undo step. Import creates one sprite per color in the image. That always reproduces the image exactly, but it may use more sprites than a hand-made OR design would.

PNG image…
The image must be exactly 16×16, use only colors from the current palette, and have only fully opaque or fully transparent pixels. If the colors need more sprites than you have room for (8 in total), nothing is imported and a message explains why.
TinySprite file…
A backup file (.tiny) from TinySprite, of type msx2. Every slot is imported in order. Slots that don't fit in the 8-sprite limit are skipped whole, and a warning tells you the import was truncated.

Error and warning messages appear above the sprites; close them with ×.

Export

Every format writes the sprites in card order (= priority order), using the same bytes the MSX2 expects: 32 pattern bytes and 16 color bytes per sprite, with the CC bit set on OR sprites. Layouts are described in the handbook, section 5.3.2.

PNG images
One file per sprite (sprite-0.png, sprite-1.png, …) plus composite.png, the combined result as shown in the preview. Your browser may ask permission to download several files.
C source
sprites.c with two arrays: sprite_patterns[] and sprite_attributes[] (the color table bytes).
Binary (.bin)
Two raw files, patterns.bin and colors.bin, ready to load into the sprite pattern and sprite color tables in VRAM.
TinySprite
sprites.tiny, one slot per sprite with that sprite's own pixels, for opening in TinySprite.
BASIC listing
sprites.bas: a runnable MSX-BASIC program. It switches to SCREEN 5, loads the patterns and colors from DATA lines, and places the sprites at their preview offsets so you can see the OR effect on a real MSX2 or an emulator such as openMSX.

Autosave

Every change is saved in your browser's local storage a moment after you make it, and restored when you reopen MORDEN2. The status bar shows Saving… (orange dot) then Saved (green dot).

This copy lives only in this browser on this computer. Clearing site data or using a private window loses it. Use Export (TinySprite is the most complete format) to keep or share your work.

Context help

Hold Ctrl in the editor: the cursor becomes an arrow with a question mark. Click any part of the screen (a tool, a menu, a sprite grid, the preview…) and this page opens at the section about it. Nothing in the editor changes when you do this.

The page opens in its own browser tab and is reused, so help never piles up in extra tabs. The ? button in the top bar opens it too.
